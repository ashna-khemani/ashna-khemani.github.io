# Real shit: college
## The rude awakening
* Originally did stuff in college with a similar study style. Never touching the textbook or really diving into the clear explanations of all the details. Mainly relying on experience
	* Frosh classes have a lot of homework. High frequency, high intensity, and highly problem-set based. Went in with the same mindset as high school, where the act of doing homework really well—learning what I needed to know to solve the problems all by myself, talking to professors/TAs if I was unsure that I would get 100%—would be enough to prepare for the exam
* Practice exams are a thing in college?! The only time I ever did a practice exam in high school was for the SAT... surely this was not that serious... I just did them to prove to myself that I kinda sorta knew enough to do well. Go to office hours if I was unsure. Also... how much would the actual exam *really* deviate from the practice one... right... *right*... *RIGHTTTT??*

I got a 42% on what I think was my first-ever exam at Penn. It was Gen Chem 1, or CHEM 1012. I was very sad. Very embarrassed (almost 2SD below the mean... ow). My life was Joever.
* Pretty quickly realized the best way to review was to actually read reference material — not just lecture slides and notes, but the textbook itself to get a hang of all the deets and they all worked together.
	* Making study guides, or extended "cheat sheets." I had realized that I had learned most effectively when making my cheat sheet, since it forced me to get a broad view of relevant ideas and how they all connected to each other.
* In the middle of slowly realizing that I had to read actual references, I'd sometimes make "Quick Notes" on things that were suddenly made very clear to me that I didn't understand before. It was nice to have this little quick reference for things that I was prone to confusing or forgetting. Like leaving a little note for future Ashna.
	* I could do this for... all the main points for a class
* Basically use the Feynman technique to act as if I'm teaching Ashna 20 Years From Now this material!
	* Not so much detail that we waste time now re-establishing the detail or terminology I will probably always remember, but just enough structure that I can pick up from scratch a few years from now.
