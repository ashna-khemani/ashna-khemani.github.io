# Real shit: college
## A rude awakening
I originally started college with a similar study style. Never touched the textbook or really dived into the explanations of all the little details. I relied on learning through experience, because freshman year classes have *so much homework*. High frequency, high intensity, and highly problem-set based. I assumed that doing the homework really well—learning what I needed to know to solve the problems—would be enough. Turns out, haha, that is [[How to Study#the humbling chem exam|not true]].

Another thing I found weird in college was practice exams?! The only time I ever did a practice test was for the SAT/AP examps. Sure this was not that serious... right...? I did them anyway for the practice, and also, ow much would the actual exam *really* deviate from the practice one... right... *right*... *RIGHTTTT??*

All in all, exams in college were pretty different, and required a more conscious and concentrated effort. I definitely worked hard and had a lot of discipline going into college, but I still felt like I wasn't getting the results I wanted. Was getting strong A's just something out of my reach? And this was just frosh year! The idea of having to take harder classes eventually was a terrifying thought...

So there was only one way to deal with it. **I had to come up with my own [[How to Study|study methods]]**. Which took a while to discover...


## Opinion: group studies
***Overall rating: 6/10***

(Calling this out in particular because I have strong feelings about it!)

Collegiate and ex-collegiate fellas (including myself) will tell you all about how they and their friends stayed up till 4am studying for a final exam in someone's dorm or a library study room. And they got cheap take-out or maybe even a couple Fireball shots to keep going.

**But tbh...**

I'm not inclined to study with others. If someone asks me to and I'm on track with my study schedule, sure! Why not, I love good company, and, hey, sometimes group study sessions are extremely productive. They're nice motivation to push yourself to start a focused task that you've been putting off for a while.

Generally though, *I like doing about 85% of my studying completely on my own*, usually in my room (yes, I know this is lowkey anti-social behavior of me). It's cringe but I like it for a few reasons:

1. My decor :). Actually makes me excited to spend time there and lock in. And it's relaxing. Feelin' like Lo-Fi Girl.
2. I can talk to myself. I can explain things out loud to myself, or articulate why I'm confused about something. Putting things into words, saying them, and hearing someone (me or someone else) say them often helps things click for me.
3. I can listen to music out loud (fun fact, I do not like feeling things on my ears)
4. I'll get a little stressed out if I'm studying concept A but my friend is ahead on concept D. My mind will go "oh fuck I know nothing." It's totally irrational, but still happens for a couple reasons (theories)
	1. I get into really focused flows really easily. I don't like getting interrupted, and get worried that I won't be able to lock back in again.
	2. People tell me I can sometimes be [[Competition|competitive]]...? Which I don't think is super true.
5. I can get up and walk around and not be self-conscious or worried about bothering someone
