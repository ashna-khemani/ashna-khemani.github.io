Book: _Difficult Conversations_, by Stone, Patton, and Heen

- Difficult convos have a common structure if you pay attention to what’s NOT said…

	- Gap between said vs. not said makes it a difficult convo

# The 3 Conversations happening

## “What happened?”
* Disagreement on what has happened / should happen. Who’s to blame? What do we do now?
* Where most of the time is spent
### Truth
- Assumption: I'm right, you're wrong. Is this a fair assumption?
- Difficult convos are not about facts, they're about interpretation and action → what is **important** 
	- Ex: both agree the brochure is wrong, disagreement is whether it matters and how to handle it if so
- 💡 shift purpose from proving you're right → understand perception, interpretation, and values of both sides. Ask questions, how is everyone making sense of world.
	- → note that this is NOT "the truth"! people tend to get tangled in what they *perceive as "truth"*; not productive

### Intentions
* People assume others' intentions. Affects how you think of them → how convo goes
* Often don't know intentions, but often assume they're bad
* Intentions are invisible but important. Assuming them can make things hairy

### Blame
- Similar to arguing about the "truth." Brings more disagreement, denial, no learning. Fear of punishment, a binary answer
- No one wants to be blamed → everyone's energy goes into defending themselves
- Situation that led to the difficult convo is almost always result of what both parties did/failed to do
- Smartest move is to figure out:
	1) what prevented you from seeing it coming
	2) how to prevent it from happening again
* 💡 explore why things went wrong, how to correct going forward
* 💡 think "contribution," not "blame"


## “Feelings”
* Are my feelings valid, do I share them, what do I do about other person’s feelings? Are they hurt?
* May not be addressed directly but they leak in

## “Identity”

* What does situation mean to me, impact to my ego and self-image? Determines if you feel balanced or anxious
